# Dot Illustration

Animals in motion drawn as a dot matrix: a low-poly swift, a butterfly and a manta ray of our own, and the three.js horse and the Khronos fox models, lit in 3D, rendered small and hidden each update, read back cell by cell and drawn flat as light-blue dots, bright-blue dots and navy pills on a 6:4 frame (1200 × 800, scaled to fit). The dots update 24 times a second in steps, like stop-motion. `<DotBird />` is the bird alone, flapping, gliding and banking on a DialKit timeline; `<DotSequence />` loops bird → butterfly → manta → horse → fox with a dot dissolve between every pair. A tab switch above the frame shows either version as **Current**, the flat colours on the grey ground, or as **Sky**: the same dots and pills in white over a sky, the tones told apart by opacity, shimmering. Both sit inside the same tracking grid with its markers, texts and pins, white with opacities on Sky and solid navy on Current.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
```

Preview: http://localhost:5193/ (bird ↔ manta) · http://localhost:5193/?view=bird (the bird alone)

Dependencies: React, `three` for the hidden 3D renders, and `dialkit` with `motion` (its peer dependency) for the tuning panels and the timeline dock, which render in development only.

## How it works

A **subject** is a three.js scene in its own studio (`src/dot/studio.ts`): a white key light at 60% with a 40% ambient (intensities × π, since Lambert reflects irradiance / π, so a white surface facing the light renders at exactly 1.0 and one facing away at 0.4); and a 26° perspective camera from the direction (−2, 2, 2.4), ahead of and above the subject, placed so a set number of units span the frame's width, divided by `SUBJECT_SCALE` (0.7). On a subject's first showing it is **calibrated** (`src/dot/calibrate.ts`): 24 poses spread over 2⅜ of its cycles are rendered (not a whole number of cycles, so the samples land all through the cycle rather than on the same phases), the box of all their filled cells is taken, and the camera zooms so that box fills 55% of the frame's width or 65% of its height, whichever comes first, and centres on it, so every animal fills the frame alike and never drifts or clips. The Dot Sequence panel's Size folder scales each animal ±30% on top. The bird and the manta are each a subject with an update that poses them for a time; the renderer samples whichever is active.

Each update the active subject is rendered into a `WebGLRenderTarget` of 4 × 4 pixels per cell, 216 × 144 for the default 54 × 36 grid, and read back with `readRenderTargetPixels`. The target clears to transparent black and the materials are yellow, so a 4 × 4 block's mean alpha is the cell's coverage and its mean red over that its brightness. A cell covered at least `emptyLight` (0.12) is the animal's. Its silhouette, the rim of cells covered less than half, is light. The body inside is dealt its tones by brightness percentile, so every animal shows all three: the darkest `dark` share (30%) navy pills, the middle `mid` share (40%) bright blue, the body's main colour, and the brightest `light` share (30%) light blue (the three are taken in proportion if they do not sum to 100). The key light comes from the top left and the front (`lightAngle` −35°, leaning 0.6 toward the camera), so tops are lit, sides mid, and undersides and far sides in shadow. Blue is kept free as a mark: a cell at least 15% covered by a blue-marked part (the manta's tail) is navy whatever its ink. A subject's highlight points (the bird's beak dot) are projected onto exactly one cell each and drawn light when that cell is empty. The materials are flat-shaded, so every facet is one tone and the navy comes in clean stripes.

The grid takes the Dot Bird panel's **Density** across the frame: `columns` (54, 24–120; the presets Chunky 30, Default 54, Fine 80 and Ultra 110 set it, and moving the slider makes the select Custom) → 22.2 px cells at 1200 px, and as many rows as fill the height at that spacing (36), centred. Everything follows the cell: the dot and pill sizes, the render target (4 × 4 pixels per cell) and the dissolve's noise, so the drawing looks the same at any density, with more, smaller dots or fewer, bigger ones; a density change re-measures the grid and the target without touching the timeline. `dotSize` (0.8, 0.4–1) sets a dot's diameter in cells, the gaps between dots, apart from the density. Light and mid cells are circles 0.8 × cell across. Dark cells are vertical pills 0.55 × cell wide with round caps, and a column's run of dark cells is one continuous pill from the top of its first cell to the bottom of its last, found afresh on every draw, so a run that a dissolve has cut splits into shorter pills; a single dark cell is a pill exactly one cell tall. The canvas keeps one backing pixel per device pixel, also when the preview scales it. Flat colours only: `#DEDFE4` ground, `#9DB4FB` light, `#004EFD` bright, `#0F1548` navy, all four dials.

The dots update at the panel's `updateRate` a second (24, 12–60), gated on `requestAnimationFrame` by `src/dot/stepper.ts`; between updates nothing is drawn, so each pose jumps to the next. The update keeps the rate's grid, which on a 60 Hz screen means 24 a second alternates two- and three-frame holds; after a stall it resyncs rather than catching up. The 3D scenes render only on those updates. The stepper also offers an `every` callback, run on every screen frame after any tick, which only the Sky version uses.

## The bird

`src/bird/geometry.ts` builds it in body units, facing −x with +y up and the wings along ±z, the +z wing nearest the camera:

- Body: a lathe of an 8-point profile, 1.3 long and 0.4 across at its widest, in 9 segments around, so it is faceted.
- Head: a sphere of radius 0.16 at (−0.68, 0.10). Beak: a 6-sided cone, 0.04 × 0.42, to a point at −1.21.
- Wings: a shoulder joint at (−0.12, 0.12, ±0.15) holding an inner blade 0.55 long (chord 0.42 to 0.36) and an elbow joint holding an outer blade 0.85 long (chord 0.34 to a 0.05 point) swept back 0.4. Each blade is two strips, the trailing one drooping 14°, so a flat-shaded wing reads as two tones.
- Tail: two feathers from the tail root at 0.58, 0.42 long to ±0.2 across, 0.09 wide at the root, a fork that pivots open and closed.
- The highlight: a point at (−1.36, 0.14), 0.15 ahead of the beak, drawn as one light dot.

Its view spans 3.6 units across the frame, aimed at (0.08, 0.14, 0.12), so in the 6:4 frame the whole flight, wings up, spread or banked, stays inside.

### Motion

The flap is one wave of `flapDuration` (0.4 s) of the flap's own time: the shoulder rises to 58° and falls to 34° below level, the elbow bends by `wingBend` (35°) × −cos of the phase, so the outer wing trails the inner one up on the way down and down on the way up, the wings sweep back 14° through the upstroke, the body rises 0.05 on the downstroke and the nose dips 3°. Each pass of the loop varies the stroke's amplitude by up to ±8% from a seeded value, so passes differ. The flap's own time runs at 1 − 0.6 × `spread` of the timeline's: as the wings spread into a glide the flaps slow to 40% speed while their amplitude fades with 1 − `spread`, and coming out they pick up speed as they grow back. That time is integrated over playback and carried across the loop's wrap (so each pass starts at a different point of the stroke); a seek restarts it at the playhead.

A glide is never still. While `spread` is up, all scaled by it: the body drifts up and down by 0.15 units (2.5 cells) on a 1.2 s wave starting at the glide clip's start, so it rises and falls once over a 0.6 s glide; the bird rolls ±4° at 1.25 cycles a second; the outer wing flexes ±6° at the elbow at 3 cycles a second; and the tail's fork opens to 10° and closes at 2 cycles a second. The wing tips and the tail move a cell or more between updates, so the grid changes every update.

The "Dot Bird" timeline is one pass of the flight, 8.4 s (21 flaps), looping, with one clip per phase, each starting where the last ends; the dock moves and resizes them:

| Clip | Time | Values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| flap | 0 → 2.8 s | `power` 0.6 → 1, ease-out: the flapping builds |
| glide | 2.8 → 4.5 s | `spread` 0 → 1 over 0.6 s (the flaps slow and the wings spread, a flap and a half), held 0.6 s (the glide, alive), → 0 over 0.5 s (the first flap back starts small and grows) |
| bank | 4.5 → 6.7 s | `bank` 0 → 1 in 0.7 s, held 0.8 s, → 0 in 0.7 s, with `spread` 0.5: the body rolls `bankAmount` (25°) and yaws 18° into the turn, wings half held, flapping at half |
| flapAgain | 6.7 → 8.4 s | `power` 0.5 → 1, ease-out: the flapping grows to full |

Each value belongs to the latest clip that has started and names it, and a clip holds its end values after it ends, so there is never a moment with nothing driving the bird. `glideEvery` (1–4) takes the glide only on every Nth pass of the loop. In the sequence the bird just flaps, at full power, with the same per-pass variation.

## The manta

`src/manta/geometry.ts` builds it from code in body lengths (nose to tail root = 1), facing −x like the bird, the fins along ±z:

- Body and fins: one sheet of 28 × 12 segments whose points are placed every update. Across the span `a` is the distance from the body (0 at the centre, 1 at a tip); the leading edge runs from the nose at −0.5 back as −0.5 + 0.5 a^1.2 and the trailing edge from the tail root at 0.5 forward as 0.5 − 0.5 a^0.8, meeting at the pointed tips, a wide diamond. The sheet rises 0.1 at the body's middle (× (1 − a)², × 4t(1 − t) along the chord), so the body is thicker than the fins.
- Cephalic fins: two blades 0.3 long and 0.1 wide at ±0.14 beside the mouth, pointing forward and drooping 0.03.
- Tail: a chain of 9 rods 0.2 long (1.8 in all) from the tail root, tapering from radius 0.014 to a point, trailing 12° below the body's plane. It is blue-marked, so it comes out as a line of navy pills whatever its thickness.

Its view spans 3.4 units across the frame, aimed at (0.05, −0.02, 0.1): a three-quarter from ahead and above, the fins' span foreshortened across the frame, the tail trailing back to the right.

### Motion

The fins carry a travelling wave: each point rises by `waveAmplitude` (0.45) × a^1.6 × sin(phase − 2.4 a − 1.1 t), where the phase runs one cycle per `waveDuration` (2.8 s), `a` is the distance from the body and `t` the position along the chord, so the motion starts at the body and travels out to the tips, which move most, and runs from the leading edge back. The body rises `bodyBob` (0.09, 1.5 cells) as the tips come down and sinks as they rise, and rolls ±`bankAmount` (5°) on a period 1.6 × the wave's, so the fins are seen from slightly different angles. The tail's joints swing `tailSway` (14°) in all, each joint 0.5 rad later than the one before, sideways with 35% of it up and down, so it trails the wave with a lag. Between updates the fin tips and the tail move a cell or more, and the grid changes every update.

## Natural movement

Every animal moves on its own cycle clock (`src/dot/wave.ts`): each cycle runs ±5% faster or slower and ±5% bigger or smaller than the last, seeded, so nothing is a perfect machine loop; the clocks advance by the dot updates' steps and restart at the playhead on a seek. Strokes are asymmetric (`asym`): the power stroke takes the first part of the cycle quickly, the recovery the rest. Parts that follow the body read the cycle a few updates late (`lagged`).

- **Bird**: the downstroke is 40% of the flap, strong, the upstroke 60%, through which the wrist folds the outer wing back 35° and the wings sweep back; the body lifts 0.06 through the downstroke and the nose dips 3°, while the head takes only 30% of that rise and pitch; the tail dips 7° three updates behind.
- **Butterfly**: 0.5 s a flap, the wings clapped up to 88° at the top of the cycle, flung open through its first 35% and closed again more slowly, the hindwings 0.7 rad behind; each flap's strength varies ±15%; every two flaps pick a new lift or drop (±0.03) and a new heading (±5°) reached smoothly over them; the body pitches up 8° through the downstroke; the antennae trail three updates behind.
- **Manta**: the wave starts at the leading edge and runs out to the tips (2.6 rad behind) and back (1.4 rad), the tips moving most; the downstroke takes 45% of the cycle; the body pitches 2.5° with each stroke; every fourth stroke is 1.6 × longer, a glide through the water; the tail's root is four updates behind the fins and each joint 0.8 rad behind the one before.
- **Horse and fox**: the clips each hold one stride (measured: each hoof touches down once per clip, and every hoof's autocorrelation period equals the clip), played at the Stride dials' pace, 0.9 s for the horse (0.5–1.4) and 0.75 s for the fox (0.4–1.2), as `clipTime = u × clipLength` for the stride clock's u; the animal stays in place, only its own animation moves. On the Sky tab the clouds stream past faster while a runner is on, 1 + 0.6 / stride times the drift (1.67 × for the horse, 1.8 × for the fox), easing there and back over 0.8 s.

## The horse and the fox

Two glTF models, in `public/models/`, loaded by `src/dot/model.ts`: every mesh gets the shared lit, flat-shaded material (the mesh keeps its morph targets or its skin, so they still animate), the scene is turned −90° about Y to face −x like the other animals, centred and scaled from the box of its rest vertices (a morph-target geometry's own box also spans the targets, far beyond the body) to a length in body units, and an `AnimationMixer` plays the clip, its time set by each dot update from the stride clock, so the gallop and the run step with the dots. A model still loading draws nothing.

- **Horse**: `Horse.glb` from the three.js examples, 15 morph targets, the clip `horse_A_`, one gallop stride over 1.5 s, scaled to 1.7 body lengths before calibration.
- **Fox**: `Fox.glb` from the Khronos glTF sample assets, one skinned mesh, the clip `Run` (one stride over 1.16 s; it also has `Survey` and `Walk`), scaled to 1.6 before calibration.

The Dot Sequence panel's Stride folder sets each one's stride time. Their tracked points are vertices picked from the rest pose by where they sit in the body's box: the nose, the ears, the hooves, the tail and the back (the key ones, each just inside its extreme and on the camera's side, so it lands on a dot), the withers, the rump, the flank, the knees and a few more; each update their positions come from the posed mesh through `getVertexPosition`, morphs and skinning included.

## The butterfly

Built from code (`src/butterfly/geometry.ts`), in body lengths, facing −x: a round head (radius 0.05) and five body segments shrinking to the tail; two antennae, thin tubes along a quadratic curve forward, up and out, with a small tip; and two pairs of wings on hinges along the body, each a subdivided sheet (8 × 5 and 7 × 4) placed every update: the forewing a triangle with a rounded outer edge (chord 0.3 √(1 − s³) along the span s, 0.85 long), the hindwing smaller and rounded (0.24 √(1 − s²), 0.55 long) with a short tail point (a Gaussian bump of 0.14 at s 0.4). A band along each wing's outer 14% and three spots on the forewing (one on the hindwing), in the navy mark just above the surface, give the wings tone. Its flight is under Natural movement above; the sheets curl at the tips (0.1 × s², lagging the stroke), and the body also drifts 0.08 up and down over 2.3 s, pitches 5° over 3.1 s and rolls 3° over 2.7 s. Checked: one full 23 s loop stepped at 1/24 s, 553 updates, no two alike.

## The dissolve

`src/dot/dissolve.ts`. Dots only switch off or on. Each cell of the shape gets a score `noise × (1 − edgePriority) + importance × edgePriority`, with `edgePriority` 0.3: the noise is smooth 2D value noise, one random value every `clumpSize` (5) cells each way blended with a smoothstep, so it comes in blotches about a clump wide rather than single dots; the importance is 1 for an outline cell (a 4-neighbour is empty) and for navy, 0.5 for a light dot inside the shape, 0 for the fill. Going out, the shape keeps the `1 − progress × (1 − 0.04)` share of its cells with the highest scores, so the flat fills disappear first and the outline and navy details stay until last, and at the end 4% of the shape remains, a few scattered dots. Coming in the order reverses: the first `emptyUpdates` (2) updates hold at 4%, then the kept share rises to 1, outline and navy first, the fill growing in clumps. The mask is cut afresh on every update from the subject's new render, so the bird keeps flapping as it breaks apart and the manta is already swimming as it builds in; the navy runs are re-merged by the drawing, so a pill splits where its cells are off. Each transition takes a new noise seed (2 × the loop pass + the transition's number, plus 1000 per press of New seed), so no two look alike. With both dissolves at 0.3 s and 24 updates a second, that is 7 steps out and 7 in.

## The sequence

The "Dot Sequence" timeline is the loop, 23 s: a 4 s marker clip per animal, bird, butterfly, manta, horse, fox, and between every pair a group of two marker clips, `toX › dissolveOut` (0.3 s) and `toX › dissolveIn` (0.3 s), whose progress is the dissolve's. The dock moves and resizes them: an animal's clip length is its time on screen, and the order of the animal clips is the loop's order, since the subject showing is whichever animal's clip or build-in started latest (`src/sequence/phase.ts`). The shape kept is all of it, a dissolve-out's falling share, a build-in's rising share after its empty updates, or, through any gap left between a dissolve-out and the next build-in, the 4% floor, so nothing is ever blank for more than the chosen empty updates. The Animals folder's toggles take an animal out of the loop: when the playhead reaches its build-in (after the previous animal's dissolve-out has played) it moves on to the next included animal's build-in. **Skip to next subject** seeks to the next dissolve-out.

## The chrome on both tabs

The Current tab draws its dots at the stepped rate as always and, every screen frame over them, the same chrome as the Sky tab (`SkyPresenter.chrome`): the tracking grid's lines easing after the subject, the markers, the name and coordinates at the markers, the overlay's pins and labels, the edge name and the data block, all from the Sky panel's settings, in solid navy (the Dot Bird panel's navy colour) and the pins' white, with no opacity anywhere, so the Current version keeps its rule; the lines' easing is motion, not fading. The Sky tab draws the same chrome white, with its opacities.

## Sky version

The tabs above the frame, **Current** and **Sky**, switch how the same component instance draws: the Current version is untouched, and the Sky version passes the Sky panel's values to `<DotBird />` or `<DotSequence />`, which keep their timeline, their shape updates at the stepped rate, the dissolve and the glide exactly as they are and only present differently. Nothing remounts, so the switch is instant and the clock runs on (checked: 2.98 → 3.46 → 4.46 s across a switch and back, the same `window.__dotBird`). The tab is the Sky panel's `View › tab`, so it persists with the rest.

- **Background** (`src/sky/SkyBackground.tsx`): a layer behind the dots, filling the box. `source` is `dummy`, `image` or `video`, `path` the file, served from `public/` (`/sky.jpg` by default; for a video set `/sky.mp4`). The dummy sky is a vertical gradient from `color` (`#2D36C8`) at the bottom to the same mixed 22% toward white at the top, with three layers of clouds drifting right to left: each layer draws a 384 × 192 tile of three-octave value noise, periodic across so it scrolls without a seam, cut softly where the noise passes `0.8 − 0.32 × cloudAmount` (soft edges 0.22 wide, fading at the tile's top and bottom), stretched to 2.6, 1.8 and 1.2 × the frame's width and 1.0, 0.75 and 0.55 × its height, drifting at 3, 6 and 10 CSS px a second × `cloudSpeed`, at 75, 70 and 60% opacity: big slow clouds far, small quick ones near. The image is covered to the box; the video is muted, looped, inline and autoplays, covered to the box. A source that fails to load falls back to the dummy sky.
- **Dots** (`src/sky/presenter.ts`): exactly the Current version's shapes, through the same geometry in `src/dot/dots.ts`: round dots `dotSize` × cell across for the light and mid tones, and the dark tone as vertical pills `navyWidth` × cell wide with round caps, a column's run merged into one, at the Dot Bird panel's sizes on the same grid. All of them are pure white, and the three tones are told apart by opacity: `light`, `mid` and `dark` in %, set together by the **Opacity variant** select, Depth (30 / 65 / 100, the shadows become the brightest white lines), Soft (20 / 45 / 80, calmer, more of the sky through it), Glow edges (100 / 60 / 35, inverted: the outline glows, the inside is quieter) or High contrast (10 / 55 / 100, the strongest separation), or one by one with the sliders, which turn the select to Custom (`src/sky/variant.ts`). With `sparkles` on (off by default) about `sparkleAmount` (1.5%) of the light and mid cells are instead a smaller dot (0.55 of the radius) in `sparkleRed` (`#FF5A4E`) or `sparkleGreen` (`#4EE07A`), chosen by a seeded hash that changes every 4 shape updates. The canvas is cleared, not filled, so the sky shows between the dots.
- **Shimmer**: a dot's final opacity is its tone's × a shimmer factor between `shimmerLow` (60%) and `shimmerHigh` (100%), from 3D value noise over the grid at `shimmerClump` (5) cells and over time at `shimmerSpeed` (0.3) cycles a second (about 1.7 s from dim to bright), squared toward bright so most of the subject stays bright; neighbouring dots sit in the same patch of the noise, so waves of light cross the subject, and a pill takes one value, at its middle, so it never looks broken. No dot goes below 1%. It is drawn on every screen frame from the held shape, so it moves smoothly while the shape still steps. Opacity animates here and nowhere else; the Current version keeps its no-opacity rule.
- **Text**: an edge text stands up the top-left corner (rotated 90°, reading bottom to top), uppercase, small and white: the Text › edge template, `OBJECT TRACKING · {grid} · {fps} FPS` by default, where `{name}` (the animal, switching the moment the subject does), `{seq}` (its place over the total), `{grid}` (columns × rows) and `{fps}` (the update rate) fill in live; empty draws nothing. In the bottom-right, three lines of tracking data in a 9.5 px monospace at 80% white: `X Y W H` are the grid box's top-left corner and size in frame pixels; `LAT LON ALT` drift around the base coordinates with the box (±0.01° across the frame, 0.25 m per pixel of width); `FRM` is the count of dot updates, `SEQ` the animal's place in the dock's order over the total, `T` the time since the view started. The numbers snap to new values once a second. Both blocks keep a 12 px margin and have toggles; the base coordinates are text fields.
- **At the grid's markers**, moving with them: the animal's name, uppercase, 10 px, letter-spaced white, horizontal just above the top line, starting 8 px right of the top-left marker; and the bottom-right marker's own coordinates, `X 1012.40  Y 0738.17` in 9.5 px monospace at 80% white, just below the bottom line, ending 8 px left of the marker. The coordinates snap to new values once a second; the name switches with the edge name, at the start of each build-in.
- **Tracking grid**: two vertical and two horizontal lines, edge to edge, around the subject. The box the lines want is the subject's **loop box**, the box of every pose of its loop from its calibration, plus `padding` (3) cells, so while one animal shows the lines barely move. A hard rule runs at every dot update: no filled cell may touch or sit outside a line; a line that would is pushed outward at once, to 2 cells beyond the outermost cell, and then settles back, 95% of the way in `return` (1.8 s) and never faster than 5% of the frame's width a second, never closer than 2 cells to the animal and never inside the padded loop box. When the subject changes (at a build-in's start, the middle of the transition) the box glides to the next animal's over 0.8 s with an ease in and out. The markers, the marker texts and the coordinates follow the same lines. A crossing that survives the push is counted and logged as a console warning; `showLoopBox` draws the loop box as a faint dashed rectangle to check against. Lines are `lineWidth` (1) CSS px rounded to whole device pixels and snapped to them, no glow, drawn after the dots so they are on top of everything; the toggle `enabled` is on by default.
- **Grid colours**, per tab: Grid Current (line `#A9B0CC`, marker, label `#6B7394`, all solid) and Grid Sky (line white, marker, label, and the lines' `opacity` 0.7); with `linkColors` (on) the markers and the marker texts take the line colour. Where the lines cross, four **markers**: squares of `size` (6) CSS px rounded to device pixels, centred on the crossing and pixel-aligned, filled white or, with `style` outline, a 1 px white outline, moving with the lines and drawn over them.
- **Extras**, both off by default: `caption`, a small italic `captionText` ("grow wings and fly") reading bottom to top with its baseline 6 px inside the right line, starting 6 px above the bottom line, moving with the grid; and `stars`, 70 small white dots scattered on a seeded layout over the sky.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the Sky version is a still frame: the bird in its still pose, the shimmer's time and the sparkles' choice fixed, the lines on their target, the text's time at zero, the clouds at rest and the video paused.

## Object tracking overlay

On the Sky tab (and on the Current tab when its toggle is on), pins and labels like live object tracking, locked to the dots, in `src/tracking/`:

- **Tracked points**: every animal carries a list of points on its model, its key points first: the bird's beak, head, eye, wing joints and tips, tail tips, flank and wing mids (`trackers` in `src/bird/geometry.ts`); the manta's nose, fin tips, edges, lobes, centre and tail joints (vertices of its placed sheet); the butterfly's head, antenna tips, wing tips and corners, body; the horse's and the fox's picked vertices (above). The Points › count slider (20) takes the first N.
- **Locked to the dots**: at every dot update, from the very same posed frame that made the dots, each point's current position is read from the model (a joint's, a sheet vertex's, or a loaded mesh's through `getVertexPosition`, morphs and skinning included), a ray from the camera tests whether something sits more than 0.15 body lengths in front of it (joints lie inside limbs), it is projected to frame pixels and snapped to the centre of the nearest filled cell within one cell of it. A hidden point, or one with no dot that near, is off for that update. Nothing moves between updates; there is no easing. Through a dissolve the pins go off and on with the dots they sit on. Checked by stepping to each animal with the overlay on the Current tab: every pin's cell holds a dot (0 off, for all five, at two times each), and the nose pin sits left of the tail pin on every animal.
- **Pins**: a white dot `nodeSize` (2.5 px) across with a thin ring of 1.2 × that radius (about 6 px).
- **Labels**: the first `Labels › count` (5) key points get `x: 1012  y: 262`, the snapped cell's frame pixels, in `color` (`#C8F04A`) 10 px monospace, 8 px right of the pin on its vertical centre, or 8 px to its left when the frame's edge is near; the numbers change only at the dot updates. The first key point also gets a 14 px square, 1 px outline, centred on it.
- **Layers**: sky → dots → pins → grid lines and markers → labels, the names and the data. On the Current tab (`showOnCurrent`, on by default) the pins and labels are drawn in solid white and the label colour, no opacity.
- The "Tracking" panel (`persist: true`, id `dot-tracking`): Overlay (tracking, labels, target box, show on Current), Points (count, node size), Labels (count, colour) and Actions (**Save to file**). Under reduced motion the overlay is a still frame.

## DialKit

- The "Dot Bird" panel (`persist: true`, id `dot-bird`): Density (preset, columns, dot size, navy line width), Tone (the coverage threshold, the light, mid and dark shares, and the light direction), Colors (the four colours), Motion (flap duration, wing bend, glide every, bank amount, update rate) and, in the bird view, Actions: **Pause** (and resume), **Step one frame** (pauses and moves the playhead one update forward) and **Save to file**. The "Dot Bird" timeline (id `dot-bird-timeline`, `loop: true`) is the bird view's clock.
- The sequence view registers the same Dot Bird panel without its actions, so Density, Tone, Colors and Motion are shared with the bird view (a value set in one shows in the other), plus the "Dot Sequence" panel (`persist: true`, id `dot-sequence`) with the folders Animals (a toggle per animal, in or out of the loop), Manta (fin span, wave duration, wave amplitude, bank amount, tail sway, body bob), Size (each animal, ±30%), Stride (the horse's and the fox's stride times), Credits (the models' credit lines), Transition (out duration, in duration, noise clump size, edge priority, empty-moment length in updates, **New seed**) and Actions: **Pause**, **Step one frame**, **Skip to next subject**, **Save to file**. The "Dot Sequence" timeline (id `dot-sequence-timeline`, `loop: true`) is its clock. The Transition panel's out and in durations write into the four dissolve clips' durations; the dock sets where the clips sit.
- The "Sky" panel (`persist: true`, id `dot-sky`), registered by the preview page and shared by both views, with the folders View (the tab), Sky (background source, file path, sky colour, cloud speed, cloud amount), Sky Dots (opacity variant, the light, mid and dark opacities, shimmer low and high, shimmer speed, shimmer clump size, sparkles on/off, their amount and two colours), Grid (on/off, line width, padding, return, link colours, show loop box), Grid Current and Grid Sky (line, marker and label colours; the Sky lines' opacity), Text (edge template, marker name on/off, data on/off, base latitude, longitude and altitude), Markers (on/off, size, filled or outline), Extras (caption on/off and its text, stars) and Actions (**Save to file**).
- Both timelines autoplay unless motion is reduced. All of it shows in development only.

Save to file, on the Dot Bird, Dot Sequence, Sky and Tracking panels alike, posts every registered panel's values and timeline's timings to the dev server, which writes `src/bird/dot-bird.tuning.json` with the sections `panel`, `timeline`, `sequence`, `sequenceTimeline`, `sky` and `tracking` (a view saves what it has registered; the other sections keep the file's values); the file loads as the configs' defaults, so a saved tuning is part of the code, and what DialKit keeps in the browser still wins on this machine. DialKit stores a timeline's clips in the browser on first load and loads that copy when the timeline registers, so before each timeline's config is built (`src/bird/dial.ts`, `src/sequence/dial.ts`) `healStoredTimeline` drops the stored copy when it is from an older `DEFAULTS_VERSION` or lacks a clip the code has (a story that grew while the browser kept the old one); the code's clips, with the saved file over them, load instead. The panels' values are never touched. This is what had the bird showing in the butterfly's slot: the stored two-animal timeline put the manta's clips where the butterfly's now sit, and a reset after registration left the running clips where they were. `window.__dotBird` and `window.__dotSequence` expose each view's `play`, `pause`, `seek`, `time`, `playing` and `duration` for scripts (the sequence's also `phase()`).

## Performance and accessibility

An `IntersectionObserver` and `visibilitychange` stop the update loop and pause the timeline while a component is off screen or the tab is hidden, and resume them (only if this paused them) when it returns. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` neither timeline plays: the bird holds one still pose, wings up (shoulder 50°, elbow −8°, sweep 4°), in both views and both tabs, with no transitions, no shimmer and no cloud drift, redrawn only when a dial or the size changes. The roots are `role="img"`, labelled "Animated dot-matrix bird in flight" and "Animated dot-matrix animals: bird, butterfly, manta ray, horse and fox"; the canvases are hidden from assistive technology. Nothing animates opacity, and nothing fades.

Two updates in a row (one step apart) with the same grid are a bug, and each component logs a console warning naming both times when it happens. Checked in headless Chrome by stepping at exactly 1/24 s: the bird view over one loop, 203 updates, no two alike; the sequence over a full 23 s loop, 553 updates, no two alike, no warnings. Measured with the chrome hidden, the five animals' loop boxes are 0.39–0.58 of the frame's width and 0.58–0.66 of its height, centred within 0.02 of the middle, and each shows all three tones (light 43–54%, bright 26–32%, navy 20–31% of its pixels). Also checked: Pause leaves the canvas unchanged over 60 frames and Step one frame advances the playhead by 0.04167 s, in both views; Skip to next subject jumps from the bird to the toManta dissolve-out and from the manta to the toBird one; New seed changes a mid-dissolve pattern at the same time; Save to file from the sequence view writes `panel` (16 keys), `sequence` (11) and `sequenceTimeline` (12) and keeps the file's `timeline`; a Grid change made in the sequence view shows in the bird view; under reduced motion two captures of the sequence a second apart are identical, and so are two of the Sky tab. With the Sky work in place, the Current tab's frames at eight seeked times through the sequence are identical, pixel for pixel, to captures taken before it.
